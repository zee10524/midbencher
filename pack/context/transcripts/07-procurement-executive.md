# Interview: Procurement Executive

- **Role:** Procurement Executive, Property Management division. Creates and amends work orders and purchase orders.
- **Location:** Head office, Gurugram
- **Date:** 6 March 2026
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE

---

*The interview took place at the procurement executive's desk in an open bay on the third floor. Two monitors were on, one with SAP and one with an email inbox. A stack of printed approval notes sat in a wire tray marked "Pending sign".*

**Procurement Executive:** Sit, sit. Sorry, there is no meeting room free on Fridays. Is it okay here? People will walk past, but nobody listens to procurement anyway. *(laughs)*

**Interviewer:** Here is fine. Thanks for making time. How long have you been in this role?

**Procurement Executive:** Five years in procurement, three in property management. Before that I was on the projects side, buying cement and steel. This is harder. Cement does not take leave at Diwali.

**Interviewer:** What does your team do in the bill process?

**Procurement Executive:** We are four people. We create the work orders for services and the purchase orders for material. Rate, period, value, GL, cost centre. When something changes, we amend it. We are not in the bill process directly. We come in when the bill cannot move. Which is often.

**Interviewer:** How is a work order set up?

**Procurement Executive:** Most of them are for a year. The property sends a requirement. Fourteen guards, so many shifts, this rate. We get quotes, finance approves the budget, we release the work order in SAP for a value and a period. After that, the property runs the contract. We do not see the bills.

**Interviewer:** And when the requirement changes?

**Procurement Executive:** That is the whole problem. The property adds two guards because the RWA asked. They keep festival staff for an extra month. A new club opens and the same housekeeping agency starts cleaning it. Nobody tells procurement. The vendor supplies, the vendor bills, and the work order is still for the old value. We hear about it only when the service entry fails or AP raises a query.

**Interviewer:** How do you hear?

**Procurement Executive:** An email from the commercial executive saying "urgent, WO amendment required". Sometimes with the vendor copied, sometimes with the property head copied, which means they are already angry. Sometimes it is AP forwarding their own query to me with one line on top: "please check". That is it.

**Interviewer:** What does an amendment need?

**Procurement Executive:** If the value goes up, a budget approval from finance. For residential, a letter from the RWA approving the extra scope. For office towers, the tenant's approval if it is recoverable. Then the approval note gets signed here, then I amend in SAP, then the property redoes the service entry. If any one paper is missing, it stops.

**Interviewer:** Can you show me one that took long?

**Procurement Executive:** Let me check. *(searches inbox)* This one. Housekeeping at a residential society in Gurugram. Extra staff for Diwali, kept till end November. The commercial executive wrote on the eighth of January. I asked for the RWA letter the same day. It came on the twentieth. Then I raised the budget note. Finance sent it back because the note said "Diwali" and the period ran into December. They wanted a revised justification. I redid it. Then the approver was travelling. Signed on the eleventh, I amended on the thirteenth of February. The commercial executive thinks procurement sat on it for five weeks. From my side it was one letter, two notes and one signature I did not control.

**Interviewer:** She did say it took about five weeks.

**Procurement Executive:** It did. I am not arguing the number. I am saying where it went. *(sighs)* Once I have all the paper, an amendment is a week. Maybe less. The waiting is for paper.

*A colleague stopped at the desk.*

**Procurement Colleague:** The diesel PO for the mall, the rate revision came. Should I amend from the first or from the date of the letter?

**Procurement Executive:** Date of the letter. Otherwise AP will query the old bills. Put the letter in the folder.

*The colleague left.*

**Procurement Executive:** See, that is the other kind. Rate revisions. The vendor sends a new rate, the property agrees on the phone, and the vendor starts billing the new rate. The work order still has the old rate. AP calls it a rate mismatch and blames the vendor. Sometimes it is the vendor. Sometimes it is us, we did not amend in time.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about a work order that expired.

**Procurement Executive:** Diwali again. A security work order at one of the societies ended on the last day of October. The renewal note was ready in the middle of the month. But that week, half of finance was on leave, and the approver was at a family wedding. The renewal got released in the second week of November. Meanwhile the October bill came, the service entry failed because the order was not valid, and the agency owner was calling everyone. I think it got paid in December. He told everyone procurement held his payment for two months. I can understand. From his side, it was us.

**Interviewer:** Is there a list of orders about to expire?

**Procurement Executive:** I run a report on the first of every month. Orders ending in the next sixty days. I send it to the property heads. Honestly, I do not know if anybody opens it. Nobody has ever replied. The balance is a different thing. I cannot see how fast a property is using up a work order unless I check each one. Forty orders per property, thirty eight properties. Nobody has that time.

**Interviewer:** What about GL codes and cost centres?

**Procurement Executive:** That happens when a property asks for a new order. The request form has a GL column. The property fills it. Many times they pick whatever was on the last order. Last year a society asked for deep cleaning of the basement. One time job. They put the housekeeping manpower GL because the same agency does both. It should have been repairs and maintenance. I released it the way they asked. Three bills later, AP queried it. Now the bills had to be reversed and the service entries done again. The property said procurement released the wrong GL. I said, you gave me the GL. Then AP said, procurement should check. *(laughs)* Everybody is right, and the bill is stuck.

**Interviewer:** How often does the GL issue come up?

**Procurement Executive:** Not that often. Two, three a month, maybe. Mostly on new orders. The bigger one is balance and validity.

**Interviewer:** Was there a month when a lot landed at once?

**Procurement Executive:** February. Every year, but this year was bad. Many residential orders were set up in a hurry last year, and the extra staff from the festival months had eaten the balance. In the second week of February I had, wait, let me count. *(opens a sheet)* Eleven amendment requests in four days. No, twelve, one came on WhatsApp and I forgot to add it. All urgent. All with vendors copied. My manager asked me why procurement is the bottleneck. I said, sir, these orders were fine in December.

**Interviewer:** You track them in a sheet?

**Procurement Executive:** My own sheet. Property, order, what is pending, who I am waiting for. SAP does not tell me who has not sent the RWA letter. I also have a sticky note on my monitor with the three amendments the CFO's office has asked about. Those go first. That is not a fair system, I know.

**Interviewer:** How do the others see procurement?

**Procurement Executive:** AP thinks we are slow. Properties think we ask for too much paper. Vendors think we are the reason they are not paid. Finance thinks we do not plan. Internal audit thinks we amend too often. I have heard all of these in the same week. Nobody asks why the scope changed without anyone telling us.

**Interviewer:** The AP lead says many queries are on the company side. Work order, GL, service entry.

**Procurement Executive:** She is not wrong. But she says it like it is all the property's fault, or ours. The properties are under pressure from the RWA. They cannot tell residents, sorry, no extra guard until procurement amends. So they add the guard and tell us later. I would do the same in their place. The order is the last one to know.

**Interviewer:** Do you also handle the vendor master?

**Procurement Executive:** We update it. GSTIN, bank details, MSME registration. There is a shared mailbox for vendor documents. *(pauses)* Honestly, that mailbox is not checked every day. Bank changes go first because payment fails otherwise. MSME certificates, if somebody sends one later, it may sit. I cannot say every one is updated.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing?

**Procurement Executive:** Tell me before the order runs out, not after. If a property adds scope, let me know that week, even on WhatsApp. And give the property a simple way to see how much balance is left. Then the amendment is ready before the bill comes, not after the query.

**Interviewer:** Anything you would not want to change?

**Procurement Executive:** The approvals. I do not like the paper either. But without the RWA letter, audit will catch us. That has happened once, and I do not want it again.
