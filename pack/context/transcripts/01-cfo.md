# Interview: Chief Financial Officer

- **Role:** Chief Financial Officer, Property Management division
- **Location:** Head office, Gurugram
- **Date:** 3 March 2026
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE

---

*The interview took place in the CFO's cabin on the fourth floor. He was finishing a call when the interviewer arrived and waved them to a chair.*

**CFO:** Sorry, sorry. Board pack for the quarter. Everybody wants one more slide. Please sit. You will have tea? Coffee? I will ask for two teas, it is faster than explaining the coffee machine.

**Interviewer:** Tea is perfect, thank you. And thanks for making the time in a board week.

**CFO:** No, this is important. I have been after this topic for a year. Let me just put my phone face down. It will still ring, but at least I will not look at it.

**Interviewer:** Can you start with why vendor payments are on your list right now?

**CFO:** Three reasons. One, the vendors are unhappy. I get calls from agency owners who supply us guards at ten properties, and they say they have not been paid for two months. Two, the MSME rule. Since the tax change, if we pay a micro or small vendor late, we cannot claim that expense in the year. That is real money now, not a vendor relations issue. Three, my central accounts team keeps telling me they are drowning.

**Interviewer:** When did a vendor last call you directly?

**CFO:** Last Sunday. Sunday morning, I was at my son's cricket match. An agency owner, very old relationship, he has been with us since the first society we handed over. He said, sir, I have paid my guards from my own pocket for two months, I cannot do it a third time. I felt bad. I called the AP lead on Monday, she said the bill was in query. I asked what query. She said she will check. That is the pattern. Every escalation comes to me and I become the post office.

**Interviewer:** How often does that happen?

**CFO:** Two, three calls a week. More at month end. My EA keeps a list now. Vendor name, property, what they said. Every Monday she forwards it to the AP lead on WhatsApp. It is not a system. It is my EA's notebook. But it works better than anything else we have, which tells you something.

**Interviewer:** How long does a bill take today, end to end?

**CFO:** Forty five, fifty days. Sometimes more. For a security agency that has to pay its guards on the seventh of every month, that is very hard.

**Interviewer:** Where do you think the time goes?

**CFO:** Honestly, head office. The properties are close to the work. They check the bill, they make the service entry, they send it. Then it sits with central accounts. Five people for eleven thousand bills a year, and every bill they type into SAP, check the GST, check the work order. That is where I want AI. Read the bill, fill the fields, flag the mistakes. My team should only look at exceptions.

**Interviewer:** When you say it sits with central accounts, is that from a report, or what you hear?

**CFO:** It is what I hear from the vendors, mostly. They say the property told them the bill went to head office. So it is with us.

*The CFO's EA knocked and came in with a cheque register for signature. He signed three pages without reading them closely and she left.*

**CFO:** See, this is also paper. We are a real estate company. We sign everything. Where were we?

**Interviewer:** Whether you have a view of how long each stage takes.

**CFO:** Not stage by stage. Each property keeps a register. We get it every week. Somebody in my team once tried to stitch them together, but the formats are different and it became a whole project. So no, I do not have one clean view. That is part of the problem.

**Interviewer:** Who tried, and what happened?

**CFO:** A management trainee, two summers back. Bright boy. He spent six weeks on it. Some properties have the date in one column, some write "recd 3/2" in the remarks. One property had merged cells everywhere. He made a nice chart for one quarter and then he went back to college and nobody updated it. I still have the printout somewhere. Let me check.

*He opened a drawer, looked through a folder, and closed it.*

**CFO:** Not here. Maybe my old cabin. Anyway, it was one quarter, and it was not complete. I would not trust it.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned queries. How often does a bill get sent back?

**CFO:** Too often. The vendors do not read the work order. They charge the wrong GST. They forget the PF challan. Then we raise a query, the bill goes back, and the clock restarts. I would say maybe one in five bills.

**Interviewer:** Is it always the vendor's mistake?

**CFO:** Mostly. Sometimes the property team makes an error in the service entry. But the root is the vendor sending a bad bill.

**Interviewer:** The AP lead may have a different view on that. Have you discussed it with her?

**CFO:** Many times. She will tell you the properties send her rubbish. The properties will tell you head office asks the same question twice. Procurement will tell you nobody tells them a work order is running out until it has run out. Everybody is a little bit right. My job is to stop listening to the blame and fix the flow. That is why you are here.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried fixing it before?

**CFO:** We tried a monthly review. All property heads, AP, procurement, on a call. First two months it was good. Then it became a complaint session and people stopped joining. We also told vendors to email a scan before sending the paper. Some do. It did not change the days. And IT did an OCR thing a couple of years back, but that was before my time in this role. Ask the IT head about it.

**Interviewer:** What about the MSME side? Do you know which vendors are MSME?

**CFO:** We should. Procurement collects the certificate at onboarding. Whether it is updated, I would not bet on it. Some vendors registered after they started with us and nobody updated the master.

**Interviewer:** Has the tax team put a number on the exposure?

**CFO:** They gave me an estimate before the last audit. It was not small. I will not quote it because it was built on the master data, and if the master is wrong the number is wrong. That is the uncomfortable part. I do not even know how wrong I am.

*His phone vibrated on the desk. He glanced at it.*

**CFO:** MD's office. One minute, please.

*He took the call, said "Yes, I will send it by six," and hung up.*

**CFO:** Sorry. Go on.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing in three months, what would it be?

**CFO:** Every bill read automatically when it lands at head office. No typing. And a dashboard for me that shows what is stuck and where. If I can see it, I can push people.

**Interviewer:** What would you do with the dashboard on a Monday morning?

**CFO:** Look at the oldest twenty bills. Call whoever is sitting on them. Right now when I ask, I get three answers. The property says it went. AP says it has not come, or it is in query. The vendor says nobody told him anything. If one screen says this bill is here since this date, the argument is over.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you would not want to change?

**CFO:** Controls. Internal audit is strict. The property must certify the work. The original bill has to be with us for GST audit. I am not going to let anyone remove a check just to go faster. I have seen what happens when a developer gets a GST notice. Six months of my team's life.

**Interviewer:** Is the original bill needed before the bill is processed, or only kept for audit?

**CFO:** That is a good question. I think the team insists on the physical copy before they book. Whether the law needs it at that point, you should ask the AP lead.

**Interviewer:** How do you feel the team is doing overall? Separate from the process.

**CFO:** They work hard. That is not the issue. The AP team sits late every month start. The commercial executives at the properties are doing two jobs. I am tired of every conversation being about who is at fault. I want a conversation about where the bill is.

**Interviewer:** Who else should we talk to?

**CFO:** The AP lead, obviously. One of the property commercial executives, maybe the Gurugram residential one, she handles two properties. A property head. IT, because they have a portal idea. And if you want, I can connect you to one of the agency owners. He will not be polite.

**Interviewer:** That would help. Last question. How will you judge whether this worked?

**CFO:** Days to pay, first. Then fewer calls from vendors. And no MSME disallowance next year. If my EA's notebook is empty for a month, I will know.
