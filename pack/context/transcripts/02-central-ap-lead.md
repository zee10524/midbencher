# Interview: Central Accounts Payable Lead

- **Role:** Lead, Central Accounts Payable
- **Location:** Head office, Gurugram
- **Date:** 4 March 2026
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE

---

*The interview took place in a small meeting room next to the AP bay. Through the glass, the interviewer could see a rack of plastic trays, each labelled with property codes, and brown courier envelopes stacked on a side table.*

**AP Lead:** Please excuse the mess outside. It is month start. Do not judge us by this week.

**Interviewer:** No judgement. Thanks for the time. How long have you led the team?

**AP Lead:** Six years as lead. Before that I was a verifier here for four. So I have seen it from the chair also. Ask me anything. Just, if my phone rings, I have to take it. Treasury is closing the Friday run list today.

**Interviewer:** Of course. Can you walk me through what happens when a bill reaches your team?

**AP Lead:** Packets come by courier from the properties. Mostly they land Friday, sometimes Monday if the courier is late. My office boy sorts them by property and puts them in trays. Each verifier has a set of properties. They pick up from their tray, open the packet and go through the checklist.

**Interviewer:** How many verifiers?

**AP Lead:** Five. One of them is very senior, she has been here twelve years. She takes the big properties and anything complicated. The other four share the rest.

*There was a knock. A woman leaned in holding a bill and a sticky note.*

**Senior Verifier:** Ma'am, sorry. This lift AMC, they have put 194C. Should it be 194J? The work order says technical service.

**AP Lead:** Let me see. *(reads)* No, it is maintenance, 194C is fine. Just write the work order line on the note so audit does not ask.

**Senior Verifier:** Okay. Thanks.

*She left.*

**AP Lead:** That is her. Everybody goes to her. The new ones also, even when the question is for me. I do not mind. She is usually right.

**Interviewer:** How long does it take to process one bill?

**AP Lead:** If the packet is complete, we post it in two days. Honestly most bills take ten, fifteen minutes to verify. The problem is never our speed. The problem is what comes to us.

**Interviewer:** What do you mean?

**AP Lead:** The work order has expired. Or the work order has no balance left because procurement approved three months but the vendor is in month five. Or the service entry is parked, not posted. Or the GL is wrong. These are not vendor mistakes. The property should catch them. But they send it anyway, and we raise a query.

**Interviewer:** The CFO said queries are mostly vendor errors.

**AP Lead:** Some are. GST is the common one. A vendor in Delhi charges IGST to a property in Delhi, or puts 12 percent where it should be 18. Missing PF and ESIC challan for manpower. But a big share is our own side. Work order, GL, service entry. I have said this in many meetings.

**Interviewer:** Do you have a number for that share?

**AP Lead:** Not a clean one. I have not counted it properly. The categories in the register are a mess, I will come to that. It is my feeling from the emails. Sir hears from vendors, so he thinks vendors. I hear from my team, so maybe I think properties. You should look yourself.

**Interviewer:** How are queries raised?

**AP Lead:** Email. To the property commercial executive, copy the vendor if it is a vendor issue. We note it in the register. Every two weeks we have a call with the properties to go through open queries.

**Interviewer:** Is email the only channel?

**AP Lead:** Officially, yes. *(laughs)* Unofficially, my verifiers are in WhatsApp groups with some of the commercial executives. If it is small, they send a photo of the page and ask, is this right. I tell them, fine, but put it on email also, otherwise there is no record. Half the time they forget. And I keep my own Excel of open queries, separate from the register. The register is for audit. My Excel is so I know who to shout at. Before the fortnightly call I print it and go through it with a pen.

**Interviewer:** Can you show me a query that took a long time?

**AP Lead:** Let me check my sheet. *(opens laptop, scrolls)* Okay, this one. Housekeeping at one of the residential societies with a club. December bill. It came to us in the second week of January. First query, the split between the club cost centre and the tower cost centre was wrong. It went back. The property redid the service entries. It came back in early February. Then the club work order had no balance, so we could not post. Second query. That went to procurement for amendment. Amendment came, it came back to us. Then my verifier found the PF challan was for November, not December. Third query. I think it got paid last week. Let me confirm. *(scrolls)* Yes, posted on the twenty sixth, paid in the Friday run.

**Interviewer:** Three queries. Could they have been raised together?

**AP Lead:** In theory. In practice when the split is wrong, my verifier stops there. Why check further if it is coming back anyway? Then it comes back and she starts again. I know it is not ideal. But if I tell them to check everything on a bill that is already wrong, the clean bills wait.

**Interviewer:** How long does a query take to close?

**AP Lead:** Depends. A missing challan, a week. A work order amendment, that goes to procurement, then approval, then the property redoes the service entry. That can be a month.

**Interviewer:** You said the property should catch these. Does the property have a checklist?

**AP Lead:** Yes. And we have ours. Ours is longer because we also check tax. But yes, many items are the same. We check again because we have found too many mistakes. If I get audited, it is my team's name on the posting.

**Interviewer:** The property heads have told the CFO they feel checked twice.

**AP Lead:** I know. I hear it in every review. They say, if you do not trust us, why do we check. I say, if I could trust you, I would not check. It goes round and round. Two years ago one property sent a diesel bill where the challan was for a different property. Signed, stamped, everything. After that, nobody in my team will skip a line. I do not blame them.

**Interviewer:** About the original bill. Do you need the physical copy before you post?

**AP Lead:** We have always worked on the physical packet. The originals are filed here for GST audit. Could we work on a scan and receive the original later? Technically, yes, I think. Audit needs the original on file, not in my hand on the day I post. But nobody has asked us to change.

*Her phone rang. She looked at it and answered.*

**AP Lead:** Haan, bolo. *(pause)* No, that one is not in the Friday list, it is in query. Tell him to call the property. *(pause)* Okay, I will send you the list by four.

*She hung up.*

**AP Lead:** Treasury. A vendor called them directly. Now vendors have found the treasury number also.

**Interviewer:** What does your week look like?

**AP Lead:** Monday and Tuesday are heavy because the Friday packets are waiting. Month start is worst. All manpower bills come together, security, housekeeping, gardening. Mid-month we are lighter.

**Interviewer:** What does month start feel like for the team?

**AP Lead:** We stay till eight, eight thirty. Saturdays also sometimes. The manpower packets are thick. Attendance sheets for sixty guards, challans, everything. My team is tired of being told we are slow when they are sitting late every month. Last year one verifier resigned in March. She told me, ma'am, every vendor call is like it is my fault.

**Interviewer:** If someone gave you AI that reads the bill and fills SAP, would it help?

**AP Lead:** It would save some typing. I will not say no. But typing is maybe ten percent of our time. Chasing queries is much more. And waiting for packets.

**Interviewer:** What would help most?

**AP Lead:** Bills that come right the first time. And knowing on Monday what is coming on Friday, so I can plan who does what.

**Interviewer:** Can I see the query log?

**AP Lead:** It is in the register, a query category and a text column. The categories are not perfect. People put a lot under Other. I have asked them to stop. They say Other is faster than reading the list. I will share the export. And my own Excel, if it helps, but do not judge my colour coding.
