# Interview: Property Commercial Executive

- **Role:** Commercial Executive, two residential societies
- **Location:** Gurugram
- **Date:** 5 March 2026
- **Interviewer:** Riklr FDE, on site

---

*The interview took place in the commercial executive's cubicle behind the facility desk of the larger society. Two lever arch files lay open on the desk, one with yellow highlighter across most pages. A printed attendance sheet was taped to the partition with names circled in red.*

**Commercial Executive:** Sorry, give me two minutes. The housekeeping supervisor is outside, he wants to know if his bill is done. *(calls out)* Bhaiya, tomorrow. I told you, tomorrow. *(turns back)* Okay. Sorry. Please sit. It is the fifth, so you have come on a bad day. Or a good day, if you want to see the real thing.

**Interviewer:** Probably a good day for us. You cover two properties. What does a normal month look like?

**Commercial Executive:** First ten days are only manpower. Security, housekeeping, gardening. Every agency sends the bill in the first week. Then the rest of the month is AMC, diesel, small repairs, whatever comes.

**Interviewer:** How do you split your time between the two sites?

**Commercial Executive:** Mornings here, afternoons at the other one, three days a week. The other two days I stay where the fire is. In the first week it is fire everywhere. I do not go home before eight. My husband has stopped asking.

**Interviewer:** Take me through one manpower bill.

**Commercial Executive:** The security agency raises the bill for, say, fourteen guards for February. Before I accept it, I need attendance. We have biometric at the gate. Security supervisor downloads it on the first. But biometric is never complete. Some guards punch in and forget to punch out. Some days the machine is down because the network is gone. And relievers.

**Interviewer:** Relievers?

**Commercial Executive:** If a guard is on leave, the agency sends a reliever for one or two days. The reliever is not in biometric. He signs the gate register. So I have biometric, I have the gate register, and I have the vendor's claim. Three numbers. I sit with all three and match person by person.

**Interviewer:** Can you show me how you do it?

**Commercial Executive:** This one. *(points to the sheet on the partition)* I print the biometric, print the vendor's sheet, and take a photo of the gate register pages on my phone because I cannot take the register away from the gate. Then I go line by line with a highlighter. Yellow means matched. Red circle means problem. This guard, see, biometric shows him absent on the ninth and tenth, vendor has charged. Gate register has a different name for those two days. So probably a reliever. But the reliever's name is spelt differently in two places. Now I have to call the agency.

**Interviewer:** How long does that take?

**Commercial Executive:** For one agency at one property, maybe half a day if it is clean. If there is a dispute, the vendor says the guard was there and biometric says absent, then I ask the supervisor to check CCTV. That can take two, three days.

*The security supervisor knocked and came in.*

**Security Supervisor:** Madam, the night shift reliever on the twenty second, his name is in the gate register. Agency is saying he was there for the full shift.

**Commercial Executive:** Did he sign out?

**Security Supervisor:** In-time is there. Out-time is blank.

**Commercial Executive:** Okay. Check CCTV for the main gate between six and seven in the morning. Send me a screenshot on WhatsApp. Not today, tomorrow is fine.

*He left.*

**Commercial Executive:** See. That is one line on one bill. This bill has, I think, eleven lines like this. No, sorry, nine. Two I closed yesterday.

**Interviewer:** Then what?

**Commercial Executive:** Then the department head signs the attendance. Then I check the bill against the work order: rate, number of guards, period. Then I create the service entry in SAP. Then the packet goes to the property head for signature.

**Interviewer:** And the work order. Does it ever block you?

**Commercial Executive:** Every month for someone. The work order is approved for a value. If the society added two guards in December, the value runs out before March. I cannot make the service entry. I write to procurement for an amendment. That takes two weeks if I am lucky. Meanwhile the vendor calls me every day.

**Interviewer:** When did that last happen?

**Commercial Executive:** Right now. The housekeeping agency at the other site. The residents' association asked for extra staff for the Diwali period, and the agency kept them on till end November. Nobody amended the work order. The December bill came on the fifth of January. I tried the service entry, it failed. I mailed procurement on, let me check *(opens her email)*, eighth of January. They asked for the RWA approval letter. I got that by the twentieth. The amendment came on the thirteenth of February. So now the December bill and the January bill both went together. The agency owner came and sat at the facility desk for one full hour in late January. He was not abusive. He was just sitting. That is worse, honestly.

**Interviewer:** Do you know in advance when a work order will run out?

**Commercial Executive:** If I check SAP every time, yes. Nobody has time to check SAP for forty work orders every week. I find out when the service entry fails.

**Interviewer:** Do you keep any tracking of your own?

**Commercial Executive:** My own Excel. Every bill, the date it came, what is pending, who I am waiting for. Colour coded. The property register is what the facility desk fills. That is for the record. My sheet is so I can sleep. I also have a WhatsApp group with all the agency supervisors for both sites. They send attendance photos there. Not official, but if I wait for official I will never finish.

**Interviewer:** Some bills are split across cost centres?

**Commercial Executive:** One of my societies has a club. Same housekeeping agency does both. The club has its own cost centre and work order. So from one attendance sheet I highlight which people were in the club and which in the towers. Two service entries. If I get the split wrong, head office sends it back.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened?

**Commercial Executive:** December. It came back for the split, then after I fixed it, it came back again for something else. I do not want to talk about that bill. *(laughs)*

**Interviewer:** When does the packet leave for head office?

**Commercial Executive:** Courier picks up on Thursday. Whatever is signed by Thursday morning goes. If the property head is travelling, it waits for next Thursday.

**Interviewer:** How often does the property head sign?

**Commercial Executive:** He tries to do it once or twice a week. I keep a folder on his table. I put a sticky note on top with the vendor names who are calling the most, so he does those first.

**Interviewer:** What do you hear back from head office?

**Commercial Executive:** Queries by email. Many are things I already checked. Sometimes they check the attendance again, which I have spent two days on. Sometimes it is fair, like a GST error I missed.

**Interviewer:** How do you find out the status after it leaves?

**Commercial Executive:** I do not, officially. Unofficially, one of the verifiers joined with me, we did training together. I message her. Is this bill there, is it in query. She tells me if she can see it. If it is not her property she asks the others. That is my tracking system for head office. One person's kindness.

**Interviewer:** What are you most tired of?

**Commercial Executive:** Being the one in the middle. The vendor shouts at me. Head office sends me queries. Procurement says it is not their priority. The property head asks why the vendor is calling him. And I am doing the attendance for four, five days every month with a highlighter.

**Interviewer:** If you could fix one thing?

**Commercial Executive:** Attendance. Give me one view where biometric, register and the vendor claim are matched, and only show me the people who do not match. That alone saves me four, five days a month. Second, tell me before a work order runs out, not after.

**Interviewer:** Do vendors ever submit bills electronically?

**Commercial Executive:** Some email a scan first and then bring the print. A lot of them send a photo on WhatsApp to ask if it is fine before printing. But the official one is paper at the facility desk.

**Interviewer:** Do you check the photo when they send it?

**Commercial Executive:** If I have time. If I see the GST is wrong I tell them, change it before you print. It saves both of us. But in the first week I cannot look at photos. I can barely look at my phone.
