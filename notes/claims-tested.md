# Claims tested against the data

Each claim in the ledger of `notes/understanding.md` (L1 to L57) and three from the AP lead (S1 to S3) is tested against the cleaned data. The claim wording is verbatim from the ledger or the transcript. Every number comes from a script and is registered in `notes/numbers.md` under the figure ids in `analysis/out/claims_tested.csv`.

```
python analysis/clean.py
python analysis/02_stage_times.py
python analysis/04_query_forensics.py
python analysis/05_claims_tested.py   # or: mingw32-make analyse
```

**Verdicts.** CONFIRMED: the data supports the central statement. PARTLY: some part holds, only a proxy can be tested, or the answer is a range. REFUTED: the data contradicts the central statement. NOT TESTABLE: the pack holds nothing that can speak to it. I added the fourth verdict rather than force a call.

**Basis.** Every row says what kind of evidence its numbers are.

- **measured**: a direct count or statistic on the thing the claim states.
- **estimated from proxy**: the number stands in for something the data does not record (elapsed days for effort, a text rule for cause, a simulated report window), and rests on an assumption stated in the row.
- **reported only**: nothing in the pack bears on it. The claim, or a survey figure, is all there is. Where a document figure is quoted it is marked as one.

**Tally of 60 claims.** Verdicts: 12 confirmed, 26 partly, 6 refuted, 16 not testable. Basis: 31 measured, 18 estimated from proxy, 11 reported only.

## What this changes

- **The CFO's two headline numbers are both too high.** A typical bill takes 37.6 days (median 35), not 45 to 50, and 13.1% of bills are queried, not one in five. His 'sometimes more' is true: 20.3% take over 45 days.
- **Head office holds about half the days, but waiting is most of it.** Of the 37.6 days, 81% is waiting and 19% is a person working on the bill. Verification itself is 1.6 days, so reading bills faster could save at most that, which is the IT head's point about the earlier OCR pilot. This rests on elapsed time standing in for effort, so it is a proxy until AP time data is seen (Questions for the client, L9).
- **The weekly courier is a hard schedule.** Every packet arrives on a Friday and the lag is fixed by the weekday of signing: a Wednesday signature arrives in 2 days, a Thursday signature in 8. That is a lever that needs no AI.
- **One verifier carries 40.3% of the bills** and their queue is 11.3 days against about 7.1 for the others, at every property.
- **The work-order and amendment stories cannot be settled from the pack.** There is no amendment date, one query per bill, and nothing on who read the 60-day report. What the data does show is that 601 orders ran past approved value, usually before they expired.

## The 10 claims with no number from the data: is there an honest proxy?

16 claims are NOT TESTABLE. 6 of them (L9, L32, L33, L34, L37, L42) carry a context number from the pack and are covered in the next section. The other 10 have no number at all. For each I asked whether anything in the pack could bound the claim without inventing a figure. None can, so all stay NOT TESTABLE and every row below is **reported only**. The last column names what is needed instead.

| L17 | "In practice when the split is wrong, my verifier stops there." | **No** | The log holds 1,441 queries on 1,441 bills (repeats: 0), and AP raises each one a mean 1.0 day after picking the bill up. ## Notes

- **Clock.** Where a claim does not say when the clock starts, the figure uses the invoice date and the desk date side by side.
- **Proxies.** Elapsed days stand in for effort wherever a claim is about time spent (L9, L10, L11, L32, L49). They give an upper bound, not a measurement.
- **Signing and posting dates.** Signature dates and posting dates are spread across all seven days of the week, which makes weekday claims about them unreliable (data-audit D15). Weekday tests use the receipt date (always a Friday) and the payment date.
- **Extract limits.** The SAP extract has no service entries, work-order lines or amendment history; claims that depend on those are NOT TESTABLE rather than guessed.
