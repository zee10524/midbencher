# Reader evaluation against the hand-labelled bills

Generated by `analysis/15_reader_evaluation.py`; every count is registered in `notes/numbers.md` under `re.*`. The labels file `analysis/gold_labels.csv` was read, not edited. Only formatting is normalised (spaces, name case, amounts as numbers, dates as YYYY-MM-DD). The label `amount` is compared with the reader's grand total.

**Two scores, one reader output.** The **FIRST-RUN score** was taken from a frozen copy of the reader's output (`analysis/reader_first_run.json`) made before the labels were used for anything, with the labels as first given; the reader was not changed to chase it. After it, the `bill-28.jpg` amount label (4,41,616.60) was found to have two digits swapped (the page prints 4,14,616.60, taxable 3,51,370.00 + IGST 63,246.60) and the user corrected it by hand. The **SECOND score** uses the corrected label on the same frozen reader output. It is not tuned: no reader change has touched a scored field (`analysis/15_reader_evaluation.py` checks the live reader against the snapshot; when a scored value or confidence differs, a separate post-fix section follows the first-run tables, labelled as tuned after seeing the first-run failures).

| Score | Labels | Field readings correct | Bills fully correct on the four key fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIRST-RUN | as first given | 52 of 60 | 5 of 10 |
| SECOND | bill-28 amount corrected | 53 of 60 | 5 of 10 |

By type (first run): fully correct bills pdf_single 2 of 3, pdf_packet 2 of 3, phone_photo 1 of 4. The correction adds one correct amount reading (photos 3 of 4) and changes no key-field bill count, because the GSTIN on `bill-28.jpg` is also wrong. Calibration under the second score: confidence 0.90 or more 44 of 44 right, 0.70 to 0.90 6 of 8, below 0.70 or not read 3 of 8 (the corrected amount was read at 0.50, so it moves from a wrong to a right reading in the lowest band). Ten bills are still far too few to call the confidence calibrated. The tables below are the first run; the second score differs from them only in that one amount.

**Reading.** 8 of 60 readings are wrong or missing. 6 of them carry a confidence below 0.70 (including the fields the reader declined to read), so the reader itself flagged them. 2 are wrong at a confidence of 0.70 or more: `bill-23.jpg` gstin (read `06DOOFO6273K1ZU`, confidence 0.80); `bill-28.jpg` gstin (read `06OUMCV9219I1ZB`, confidence 0.80). Those are the dangerous cases: a wrong value that is not flagged as unreliable. Both are GSTINs read from phone photos, at the 0.80 level that the reader gives when the shape is valid and the state agrees.

**Ten bills are far too few to say whether the confidence is calibrated.** Each band holds only a handful of readings, one reading moves a percentage by several points, and the fields, the vendors and the photos are not a random sample. What can be said is narrow: on these ten, the 0.97 amounts were right every time, the 0.95 and above GSTINs were right every time, and the 0.80 GSTINs were right only some of the time, so 0.80 looks too high for a GSTIN read from a photo. Calibration needs the 40 labelled bills at the least, and a different set from the one used to tune.

## Effect of the post-fix reader on all 40 bills

The post-fix reader renders pages at the same 300 dpi (400 dpi was tried and made amounts worse, so it was reverted), reads the header again on its own, and, where the GSTIN is unclear, repairs one confusable character or looks the vendor up by name in the vendor master, marking the result `repaired` or `vendor_master`, never `read`. The GSTIN check digit was not used: none of the 900 GSTINs in the vendor master passes it, so it would reject every true GSTIN. Same checks, same register, before and after.

| Measure, all 40 bills | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Required fields (7 per bill) not found or below 0.70 confidence | 24 | 13 |
| Bills the reader passes with every field at 0.90 or more | 14 | 21 |
| Bills with every required field read at 0.70 or more (a verdict under the old all-or-nothing rule) | 26 | 33 |
| Bills on which all six checks could run | 27 | 29 |
| Bills on which at least one check could run (a verdict) | 40 | 40 |
