# Adversarial review (4.2)

Four reviewers in turn, five objections each, written to hurt. Each one says what was done: **fixed** (in code, with a test), **partly fixed**, or **open** (moved to `notes/assumptions.md`, *Known limits*). Figures are registered in `notes/numbers.md` under the ids shown.

## 1. The AP lead: "Would I skip my own check on this verdict?"

1. **No, and the numbers say I should not.** Only 1 of the 40 inbox bills gets auto-clear (`ir.all.route.auto-clear`); 35 are human-look (`ir.all.route.human-look`). The product removes the *finding* work, not the *checking* work. *Open*: this is what the design says, but the pitch must not suggest the AP lead can skip a check.
2. **"Only 3 of 10 fully trusted" is the good news, and it is not proof either.** The two readers saw the same image, so a smudge fools both. Zero trusted-and-wrong fields (`rc.comb.trusted_wrong`) among the trusted ones has a 95% upper bound of about 6% per field by the rule of three (`rc.comb.trusted_wrong_upper95_pct`). *Fixed in wording*: the card and the comparison note now say agreement is not proof and the checks against SAP still decide.
3. **What I see is not what you measured.** The cross-check was scored on Tesseract's *first-run* reading; the app shows the *tuned* stored reading, tuned on those same 10 bills. *Open*: the measured system and the shown system differ until the readers are scored on bills nobody tuned on.
4. **Most inbox bills have no vision reading at all** (the free tier ran out: `rc.free_tier.requests_per_day_per_model`). On those the card says "Not read by the vision model yet". *Open*: the cross-check protects 10 bills, not the inbox.
5. **A typed value gets confidence 1.0 and can lead to an auto-clear, with one person and no second look.** *Partly fixed*: a reviewer name is now required and every review is logged (see the auditor). *Open*: no maker-checker rule.

## 2. The internal auditor: "Show me the trail."

1. **Human reviews lived only in a git-ignored working copy that a re-run overwrites, with no reviewer and no way to see what was typed over what.** *Fixed*: `app/ui/audit.py` writes one hash-chained line per review to `app/data/audit_log.jsonl` (reviewer, file hash, typed value, the reader's original, the vision value, the verdict after), and the reviewer's name is required. *Open*: the log is a local file that someone with access can rewrite whole; the name is typed, not authenticated.
2. **"First run, before tuning" rests on a snapshot I made.** `analysis/reader_first_run.json` is frozen, but the only proof that it predates the labels is the git history. *Open*: no external timestamp.
3. **There are two "first run" scores for the same reader.** 52 of 60 on the labels as first given (`re.first.all.fields_correct`) and 53 of 60 on the labels after the bill-28 correction (`rc.tess_first.fields_correct`). The correction was made by the person who owns the labels, after the first score, and the file was overwritten in place; the original value survives only as a constant in the evaluation script. *Partly fixed*: both numbers are now named and explained (`notes/number-audit.md`). *Open*: a label change log.
4. **The vision cache stored a normalised result, not the model's own answer.** Confidences were clamped and nulls zeroed, so the normalising cannot be audited for the 30 readings already stored (`rc.vision.successful_reads`). *Fixed going forward*: new readings keep the model's raw answer (`model_answer`). *Open*: not recoverable for the 30 stored.
5. **The 20 readings by the first model sit in the repository, unscored, next to the 10 that were scored.** Per-model folders and the model name in every file keep them from mixing, but a reader of the repo has to be told which is which. *Fixed*: `notes/reader-comparison.md` says which model was scored and why.

## 3. The head of IT: "What does this need, where does the data live, and what did you just do?"

1. **You sent bills to Google.** 30 stored readings over 24 distinct files (`rc.vision.successful_reads`, `rc.vision.distinct_files_read`) went to the Gemini free tier, and more uploads were attempted and rejected (a 404 run, then quota errors), so assume every one of the 40 inbox files left the machine at least once. The pack says to treat its data as real client data, and the head of IT said to keep this "inside something we can control and audit". The free tier may use inputs to improve Google's products. **This hurts.** It cannot be undone. *Partly fixed*: the command now refuses to send anything without `--confirm-send` and says why; the disclosure is in *Known limits*. *Open*: a real deployment needs a paid no-training tier, a data-processing agreement, or an in-house model.
2. **The key is a plain file on a laptop.** `.env` is git-ignored, no commit has ever contained it, and a test now fails if anything that looks like a Google key is in a tracked file. *Open*: it is still plaintext, on a machine, with no rotation; rotate it after use.
3. **The web app had no login, and a web page open in the same browser could post to it.** *Fixed*: it now answers only requests addressed to this machine and from its own page (403 otherwise), tested. *Open*: still no login; uploaded bills stay in `app/data/uploads` with no retention rule.
4. **Nothing has touched SAP.** The "SAP" is a SQLite file. A real connection needs an RFC read user, a way to read work orders, service entries and posting status, a decision about master-data quality (the CFO distrusts the master), and, for any write, a project with the SAP partner and audit sign-off (the head of IT's own estimate is months). *Open*.
5. **WhatsApp is the easy part on screen and the hard part in life.** It needs the business API on the company number, approved templates in each language (the Hindi here is an unreviewed draft), recorded opt-in, files stored on the company side, and a way to know the person replying is the vendor (a status sentence names an invoice and a date). The simulation hides delivery failures and wrong numbers. *Open*.

## 4. The sceptical CFO: "Show me the days saved, and what if you are wrong?"

1. **The saving is a range between assumptions, not an interval.** From a mean of `l.base.desk_mean` days the replay gives `l.lever.low.save` to `l.lever.high.save` days off (best, middle and worst case are scenario settings, not confidence). There is no rupee figure: no interest, no MSME penalty, no working capital, and no build or run cost to set against it. *Open*.
2. **The biggest lever rests on a Treasury rule nobody confirmed.** "Pay on the first run after posting" assumes the run list closes 0, 1 or 2 days before the run. If the real constraint is approval or cash, that lever is worth nothing, and the combined range without it is **not computed**. *Open*.
3. **The "running dry" headline moves a lot with one choice.** 234 orders with the last 90 days (`rw.dry`); 172 with 60 days (`rw.sens.window_60d.dry`), 273 with 120 (`rw.sens.window_120d.dry`); 171 to 275 if the horizon is 30 or 60 days instead of 45. *Fixed*: the control tower now shows the 60 and 120 day counts beside it, from the register. *Open*: no seasonality, and orders with no billing in the window cannot be flagged at all.
4. **The 0/1/2 day payment-run cut-off is a guess, and the tower's "will be late" depends on it.** The default is 1. *Open*: unconfirmed (`notes/payment-lag.md`).
5. **A vision reader on a free tier of `rc.free_tier.requests_per_day_per_model` requests a day per model cannot run the inbox**, and a paid price per bill has not been looked up. The reader's "60 of 60" is ten bills (`rc.vision.fields_correct`), read once. *Open*: no unit cost, no volume test, no statement stronger than "no error found on ten bills".
